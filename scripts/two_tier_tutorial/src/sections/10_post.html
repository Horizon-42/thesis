10 The post-training

The prior learned to imitate. But a good imitation is not the same as a skill: the prior must reach the runway with its own words. The post-training trains the prior again in a closed loop. The prior speaks, the executor flies, the judge decides, and the reward comes from the outcome.

10.1 One stage, one commanded aircraft, a window of traffic

The closed loop is a window of recorded traffic with one commanded aircraft (D29). The model speaks for that aircraft. The other aircraft fly their records. A window starts at row 0 of the commanded aircraft. It ends where the commanded aircraft is done, or when it loses separation that it answers for (D93). The usual length is about 20 minutes, but it is not a bound.

The start model is the base model with a traffic attention whose output is zero. There is no single-aircraft stage, for three reasons. A window with no other aircraft is exactly single-aircraft free generation. A single-aircraft reward does not see when an aircraft lands, so it does not limit the spread of the landing times. And the model must learn to land one time, in the loop where it lands. On the train days, 60 % of the windows have no leader in the air at their first predicted step.

Windows. A round draws real windows and augmented windows in equal counts (D100). Window A inserts one aircraft that flies its record. Window D moves the aircraft ahead. Window B moves the start of the commanded aircraft: a turn about the airport of up to ±15°, a change of height of up to ±300 m and a scale of the speed of 1 ± 0.1 (D123). The four kinds are drawn from the same flights of a round, and a real window and its A, B or D go to different batches (D124). A window that opens inside a loss of separation is left out of the draw (D113).

10.2 The reward

The reward comes only from the outcome (D30). It is 1 for a landing without a go-around on a runway of the airport’s present landing direction. It is 0.9n for a landing after n go-arounds. It is 0 for every other outcome, and for every loss of separation. There is no payment for a go-around without a landing.

A go-around then pays when it turns a probable failure into a probable landing. With the same chance of landing before and after, it gives 0.9·p, which is less than p. A go-around that prevents a loss of separation keeps the chance of 0.9. So the same reward also teaches the go-around for traffic. “Go-around” is permitted at any row after the first predicted step (D10), and the chance p is low on many flights at the start. A payment would teach a go-around where the model is not sure, not where the approach is unstable. Before the training, the design measures whether the base model says “go-around” by itself on the final. If it does, the training needs no probes.

10.3 Branch training

One reward for a whole sentence gives every word the same advantage. This is poor credit assignment. Branch training (D37, D94) gives the advantage only to the words after a branch point.

  1. Each training window is spoken one time: the first sentence, with its own random numbers.
  2. If its reward is 1, the window gives no sample. A group whose rewards are all equal gives no gradient.
  3. If the reward is less than 1, find the event time t_E: the step of the loss of separation, of the judged outcome, or of the time limit. The branch points are the first predicted step and every 120 s after it, before t_E.
  4. A second pass speaks these windows again with the random numbers of their first sentences. At each branch point it copies the state K = 8 times: the executor’s loop, the speaker (cache and mask state), the separation judge and the window’s own state. Each copy continues with its own random numbers.
  5. A branch group is the first sentence and the 8 continuations of one branch point. They differ only after the branch point. The advantage of a sentence is its reward minus the mean of its group. It applies only to the words after the branch point.

Why this design? (1) Cost. In the earlier design, speaking took about 55 % of a round, and only 37–47 % of the sentences carried a gradient. Branch training spends the extra sentences only where the first sentence failed. A continuation flies only the part after its branch point. (2) Credit. Only the words after the branch point differ inside a group. (3) Branch points. A readout of the earlier design found that a new sentence from the start rescues the most events; from 120 s before the event, fewer; from 60 s or less, few. The branch points are fixed before the run, at the start and at fixed times.

A test in the code checks that a continuation with the random numbers of the first sentence says the same words and flies the same states, within 1e-6 m. The probabilities can differ in their last bits when the batch differs. A window that differs is counted, reported, and gives no sample in the round.

A second method: train on the landed sentences (D165). A measurement of the ceiling found that the model can say a sentence that lands in almost every window (99 % in at least one of 32 draws), but it gives that sentence too little probability. The clipped surrogate of branch training did not raise it further. So this method has no advantage and no clipped ratio. Each window is spoken several times by the model of the round. For each window, the sentence that landed with the highest reward is kept; a window with no landing gives nothing. The loss is the negative log-likelihood of the commanded aircraft’s words in the kept sentences, teacher-forced under its traffic, with the same data term and the same pull to the base model. Choosing the best sentence at speaking time is not wanted: only what the model is trained to say counts. Figure 12 draws the loop with branch training and Figure 17 the loop with this method.

10.4 The loss

The loss has three parts: the clipped-ratio surrogate (ε = 0.2) with the advantage of each branch group, the pull to the base model (weight 0.04, a KL estimate on the sampled words), and the teacher-forced data term (weight 1) on single-aircraft samples of the sentences in the selection landed. The traffic attention has its own learning rate.

  • The words are scored under the speaker’s records of the permitted words, so the loss reads the same masked distribution that the speaker drew from.
  • The ratio of a word is its probability under the model over its probability under a frozen copy of the model that spoke the sample. It is computed word by word.
  • Every counted row weighs the same: the sum over a batch is divided by the counted rows of the batch (D115).
  • Dropout. The surrogate and the pull run with dropout off, because the speaker spoke with dropout off. The data term runs with the dropout of the base’s training (D107). With dropout on, in a check on random weights, the ratio was off by 3 % on average and 22.5 % of the rows fell outside the clip band.

10.5 Traffic: tokens, attention and masks

The other aircraft of a window are every other flight of the airport and the split that is in the air at that step. They fly their stored signals. Their motion is the displacement in the 2 s before the row, and their R is the runway of their record from their own first predicted step on.

  • Edge features. For each other aircraft: where it is in the commanded aircraft’s frame (front, left, height difference); its place on the approach clock and whether the two can be compared; the relation of the two runways in force (same, single, dependent, independent, unrelated); the closing rate; the closest point of approach (time, horizontal distance, height difference); the distance that the rules require between the two; and a flag for an unknown motion. Every scale is a constant in SI units.
  • Traffic attention. At each layer of the prior, a module reads the other aircraft of the step (D98). It is explained below, with Figure 19.
  • Established on the final. A function of one row: G false, the aircraft inside the region of the final of R, and its track within 20° of the course (D92). After “go-around” the aircraft is not established until a runway word ends G.
  • Separation judge. It uses the radar minima of FAA JO 7110.65BB with the rules for visual approaches. Parallel runways at least 2,500 ft (762 m) apart are free once both aircraft are turned in. An event of a window is the first loss of separation for which the commanded aircraft answers.
  • Speed-word mask. A mask of the caller on the speed column. It acts only when the aircraft and the one next ahead are both established, and while the aircraft is more than 5 NM from its threshold. It is a stated approximation: constant rate, no turn, no wind. It acts in a band that is only 0.1–2 km wide at most runways (D101).

There is no mask for the spacing on the final. The reward (0 for a loss of separation) teaches it. A mask is designed only if the readouts show that the losses at the join dominate.

The traffic attention in detail

The base prior sees only its own aircraft. In traffic, the commanded aircraft must also react to the others. The traffic attention is the module that lets it. It is a small attention, added at each of the 4 layers of the prior (the “added module” of Figure 18b), that lets the row read the other aircraft of the same instant. Figure 19 shows it in two parts: the tokens are made once for each step, and then each layer reads them with its own attention.

Part 1. The tokens: one for each other aircraft, made once for each step

A token is not the state of the other aircraft. It says what the other aircraft is to the commanded aircraft, in 20 features. All of them are computed from the recorded state of the other aircraft, before the training, and none uses a row after the step. 16 are edge features (a relation between the two aircraft). 4 describe the motion of the other aircraft. Horizontal distances are scaled as asinh(d / 5,556 m), where 5,556 m is 3 NM, the radar minimum (the scale is linear for short distances and compresses long ones).

So the 20 features are 3 + 2 + 5 + 1 + 3 + 1 + 1 = 16 edge features, and 4 motion numbers. The ground speed and the vertical rate are the 2 s displacements before the row, as for the commanded aircraft (9.1). The edge features are built from the same quantities as the separation rules and the speed-word mask (the approach clock, the relation of the runways, the required distance). So the module gets what it needs to learn to avoid a loss of separation, with fixed physical scales and without any name of an airport.

The traffic token network turns the 20 features into a vector of d numbers: Linear → GELU → Linear (20 → 64 → d), as the token network of the candidates in 9.2, but with its own weights. The tokens are made once for each step, and all the layers read them (D116). A token depends only on the other aircraft and the step, and not on the layer, so there is nothing to compute again in a later layer. A token network in each layer would also need about 9 GB at the formal size (configuration A), more than the GPU has.

Part 2. The attention in each layer

Each of the 4 layers has its own attention, with its own weights. It is built like the candidate pool of 9.3, but it looks at the other aircraft and not at the runways:

  • The query comes from the row in this layer, after a LayerNorm. The row has changed since the layer before, so each layer asks a different question.
  • The keys and the values come from the tokens, through the layer’s own Linear layers. So each layer reads another side of the same tokens.
  • It has 4 heads, so that each head can look for something different in the other aircraft. The weights sum to 1 over the real other aircraft.
  • The result goes through a last Linear layer and is added to the row. The 4 layers thus add 4 different results to the row.

Why the output starts at zero. The last Linear layer of each module starts with all weights 0. So at the start of the post-training, the output is exactly 0, and the model says exactly what the base says. Where a row has no other aircraft, the output is 0 for any weights, so a window with no traffic is the same as free generation (D29). The model can only learn to use the traffic. It cannot lose the base.

What it does not do. The other aircraft are not run through the prior. A token comes from their recorded state, and not from a hidden state of the network: a hidden state would change with each round of the training, and it would need words said to that aircraft, which a recorded flight does not have (D98). The order of the other aircraft does not matter, and their number does not matter.

Size. With 64 hidden numbers in the token network and 4 heads, the module has 1,072,704 parameters: the token network 17,984, and 263,680 in each of the 4 layers. The prior then grows from 3,805,101 to 4,877,805 parameters. The module has its own learning rate, 1e-4 against 1e-5 for the rest of the prior.

10.6 What the earlier design measured

These numbers come from the multi-aircraft readouts of instruction-v3. They are evidence, not a result of the new design. A prior of that design, post-trained with one aircraft commanded, landed between 111 s earlier and 121 s later than its record on the select days (p10, p90). A loss of separation with the recorded traffic ended 11.5 % of its sentences. Most losses were on vectored approaches (21.6 %, against 4.0 % for straight-in). The selected round of its training had 1.75 points fewer losses on the validation days (11.1 % → 9.3 %). No readout has the base model of the new design in this loop.

10.7 Stage D: every arrival of a window is commanded

In stage C, one aircraft is commanded and the others fly their records. They do not answer when the commanded aircraft does something. Stage D is the optional later stage that removes this limit: the model speaks for every arrival of a time span at one time. Each aircraft flies its own words, and all of them must keep their separation from each other and from the recorded traffic. It is one controller for a whole stream of arrivals. Stage D starts from a round of stage C that the user chooses, and it pulls to the base model, as every stage of the post-training does. It is built on the code of stage C: where one commanded aircraft becomes several, the code is made general, and with one commanded aircraft it gives stage C’s words, states and rewards again, bit for bit.

The window and one step of the loop

A window is an anchor flight and every flight of the same airport and split whose first row is within a span L after the anchor’s first row. All of these are commanded. Every other flight in the air flies its record. L is 5, 10 or 20 minutes, mixed in the training data; the model never reads L. A compressed window moves every commanded aircraft except the anchor earlier, by a factor between 0.8 and 1 on its offset from the anchor. This puts the aircraft closer to each other, so that the model has more to do.

The aircraft of a window join one loop at their own times. At each row of the loop (one row is Δ = 4 s for every aircraft):

  1. The aircraft whose first row is now join. Before its first predicted step, an aircraft’s rows are its observed rows.
  2. Each commanded aircraft gets its tokens: one for each other aircraft (9.2 and 10.5). The token of another commanded aircraft also says what the model told it: that it is commanded, that it is silent, and the words that are in force for it.
  3. All the commanded aircraft speak at once, from the state at the start of the row. No aircraft reads a word that another one says in the same row. The executor then flies each aircraft for one row.
  4. The separation judge looks at every pair. An aircraft that answers for a loss of separation becomes silent.
  5. A commanded aircraft that has landed adds its landing to the landings that the others see.

The window ends when every commanded aircraft is done or silent.

The reward

Each commanded aircraft earns the reward of stage C: 1 for a landing without a go-around on a runway of the present landing direction, 0.9n after n go-arounds, and 0 for every other outcome and for a loss of separation that it answers for. The reward of a window is the sum: W = r1 + r2 + … The recorded aircraft add nothing.

  • Why a sum. The model is one controller, and its task is that every aircraft lands safely. An aircraft that lands because another one made room for it is the controller’s success. With a reward for each aircraft alone, a leader that keeps its speed up for its follower would gain nothing.
  • Why not a mean. A landing is worth the same in a window of one aircraft and in a window of six. A mean would make each aircraft of a busy window worth less, and the busy windows are where this stage learns.
  • What it leaves out. No term for the time that an aircraft takes, no shaping by the margin of separation, and no payment for a go-around.

An aircraft that answers for a loss of separation is not removed: it becomes silent. It flies on its words in force, and the others must keep their separation from it. If it were removed, the others would get the free space, and a loss would pay the window. A loss with a recorded aircraft is charged to the commanded aircraft when the rules make it responsible, and also when only the recorded aircraft is responsible but the two records kept their separation at that step: then the model made the loss.

Credit: one aircraft is varied

A word is chosen by a random number u that the caller gives (9.4, Figure 21). In this stage each commanded aircraft has a stream of numbers of its own, made from the seed, the round, the place of the window in the round and the place of the aircraft in the window, so that the number of aircraft in a window does not shift the numbers of an aircraft.

The sum W mixes the effects of all the aircraft. If every aircraft drew new random numbers in every sentence, nothing could be said about who caused a change of W. So a branch group varies one aircraft. Figure 23 shows, as a tree, everything that the training makes from one window.

How to read the tree (an example: three commanded aircraft, v1 and v2 failed, v3 landed, so W = 1):

  1. Column 1: the window and its first sentence. The first sentence is the one that the first pass spoke. Its outcomes are v1 lost, v2 failed, v3 landed.
  2. Column 2: who is varied. One group is made for each aircraft that did not earn 1: v1 and v2. v3 landed with reward 1, so it is not varied: there is nothing to improve.
  3. Column 3: the branch points of that aircraft. They are its first predicted step and every 120 s after it, before its end. Each pair (aircraft, branch point) is a group of its own. The figure opens only b2 of v1. The dashed boxes are groups that are made the same way.
  4. Column 4: the sentences of the group. The group is the first sentence and the K = 8 continuations (three are drawn). To make a continuation, the training copies the state of the whole window at the branch point. From there, only v1 gets new random numbers. Every other aircraft keeps the numbers of its first sentence. Each row shows the outcomes of the three aircraft, then W (the number of landings here), then the advantage.

The advantage of a sentence is its W less the mean W of the group (here 1.25). It is applied to v1’s rows after b2, up to its end, and to no other aircraft’s rows. The other aircraft’s outcomes still count in W, because v1’s words reach them: all aircraft are spoken by the model, and if v1’s new words change the situation of another aircraft, that aircraft’s probabilities change (through its tokens and its masks). Its old random number may then fall in another word. It answers v1. These are the circled aircraft: in continuation 2 v1 fails again but v2 lands, and in continuation 3 v3 fails. A change of W is thus caused by v1’s words, directly or through the others’ answers, and this is a controlled comparison: only v1’s luck differs inside a group.

In the example, continuation 1 (v1 lands) and continuation 2 (v2 lands because of what v1 said) have a positive advantage, so the words of v1 that led to them become more probable. Continuation 3 (v3 also fails) has a negative advantage, so the words of v1 that led to it become less probable.

Figure 22 draws what one such group looks like in time, for another window (made-up lines). Each panel is a time–distance diagram: the horizontal axis is time, and the vertical axis is the distance of an aircraft to the threshold, so an aircraft is a line that goes down to the threshold when it lands. A steep line is a fast aircraft. The gap between two aircraft at one time is the vertical distance between their lines, and lines that cross mean an overtaking. In the first sentence, v is too close behind aircraft 2 (a loss) and becomes silent (dotted). In continuation 1, v slows down, the gap is enough, and aircraft 3, whose numbers did not change, slows down too because it would be too close behind v. In continuation 2, v speeds up and overtakes aircraft 2, and aircraft 3 speeds up and ends too close behind aircraft 2.

Which aircraft, and where. As in stage C, the extra speaking is spent only where the first sentence failed. A window whose W equals its number of commanded aircraft gives no sample. Otherwise the varied aircraft are each aircraft with a reward below 1, and each commanded aircraft of a loss of separation that one of them answers for. The branch points of v are its first predicted step and every 120 s after it, before v’s end. A group whose W is the same in all its sentences gives no sample.

One round

A round draws the windows, speaks every window once (the first pass), reads the reward W, and speaks again only the windows with W below their number of aircraft (the second pass, which varies one aircraft at a time). The loss is the loss of stage C, unchanged: the clipped-ratio surrogate with the advantage of each group, the pull to the base model (weight 0.04), and the teacher-forced data term on single-aircraft samples. The first formal campaign draws 1,000 real and 1,000 compressed windows for each round, of 5, 10 and 20 minutes in equal parts, runs 8 rounds, and starts from round 5 of the stage C run with credit by 60 s segments. Its selection readout is a fixed set of real windows of the select days, spoken with the same random numbers in every round, so that the rounds can be compared. The validation days are read one time, for the round that the user chooses.

A later version. Version 2 would add a module at each layer through which a commanded aircraft reads the hidden states of the other commanded aircraft at the same row, that is, what the model is about to say to them. Its output starts at zero. It is designed and not built.
