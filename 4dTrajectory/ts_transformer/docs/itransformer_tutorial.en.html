1. The setting: what is actually being predicted

Before any architecture, be concrete about the task. This package forecasts the remainder of an aircraft's approach from a short window of observed track. 本项目的任务：给定一小段已观测的航迹，预测接下来的进近轨迹。

The aircraft's state is expressed in a local ENU frame anchored at the runway threshold (以跑道入口为原点的本地东-北-天坐标系), so the numbers are metres from the touchdown point rather than degrees of latitude. Six numbers per sample:

channels.py:90 — the CHANNELS tuple config.py:45–61 — dt_s, seq_len, pred_len
ChannelMeaningNotes
e, n, ueast, north, up (metres)Position relative to the threshold. At touchdown all three are ≈ 0.
edot, ndot, udottheir time derivatives (m/s)Named *dot, not v*, because they are chart derivatives: ∫ edot dt reproduces e exactly by construction. Remember this — section 9 is about the model discovering it.

Samples sit on a uniform 2-second grid (dt_s = 2.0). The model sees seq_len = 60 samples of history — 120 seconds — and predicts pred_len samples ahead. There are two horizon modes:

window mode

pred_len = 30 → 60 s ahead. Short hop. To reach the runway you call the model repeatedly, feeding its own output back in (forecast.recursive_forecast).

full mode

pred_len = 300 → 600 s ahead. One shot, covering the entire remaining approach for 97.8 % of flights. Short approaches are zero-padded and masked out of the loss.

So in tensor terms, stripped of all aviation meaning, the model is a function:

# B = batch size, L = 60 lookback steps, C = 6 channels, H = 30 or 300 horizon steps
model(x: Tensor[B, L, C]) -> Tensor[B, H, C]

itransformer/window: in (4, 60, 6) -> out (4,  30, 6) | 409,374 parameters
itransformer/full  : in (4, 60, 6) -> out (4, 300, 6) | 444,204 parameters

That single-signature interface is imposed by this project, not by upstream — the two vendored models disagree about how they are called, so each gets a thin adapter. models.py:26–41

2. The token question (the whole idea)

A Transformer does not know about time, or channels, or aircraft. It knows about a set of tokens (令牌 / 标记) — a bag of vectors — and its one trick is to let every token look at every other token and update itself accordingly. That trick is called attention.

So to apply a Transformer to anything, you must answer one design question first:

The only question that matters

Given a table of numbers with a time axis and a channel axis, what should one token be? 给定一个「时间 × 变量」的数值表，什么才应该是一个 token？

Our data really is just such a table: 60 rows of time, 6 columns of channel. Picture it as a grid. There are two obvious ways to cut it up.

Why the vanilla choice is awkward for this data

The vanilla choice is inherited from language, where it is obviously right: a sentence is a sequence of words, a word is a meaningful unit, so word = token. Time series break the analogy in three ways.

  1. A single time step is not a meaningful unit. The word “runway” carries meaning by itself. The reading (e=-8420, n=3110, u=740, ė=-71, ṅ=26, u̇=-3.4) at one instant carries almost none — what matters is how it is changing, which lives across many steps. Attention is being asked to compare units that individually say little.
  2. It mashes incompatible physical quantities into one vector. That column contains metres and metres-per-second, east and vertical, mixed together and projected by a single small matrix into one token. Each channel has its own scale, its own noise, and its own physics; the vanilla embedding blends them before any layer can reason about them separately. 不同物理量、不同量纲被强行压进同一个向量。
  3. Adjacent time steps are nearly identical. At 2 s spacing, consecutive samples of a smooth flight path differ by a fraction of a percent. Attention weights over 60 such tokens tend toward mush — there is little for “which time step should I look at?” to latch onto, whereas a plain linear layer over the whole series handles smooth trends perfectly well.
Historical note

This was not a theoretical worry. Around 2022 a widely-noted paper (“Are Transformers Effective for Time Series Forecasting?”) showed that a single linear layer beat a stack of elaborate Transformer variants on standard forecasting benchmarks. iTransformer is one of the answers to that embarrassment: keep the Transformer components exactly as they are, and change only what a token is. The paper's own framing is that no module is modified — 不改任何组件，只改「什么是 token」。

That last row is the subtle one and worth pausing on. If attention no longer runs along time, what models the temporal dynamics? Answer: the feed-forward network. Each token is a whole 60-step series compressed to 128 numbers; the FFN transforms that representation. So the division of labour swaps cleanly:

Attention now does

cross-channel correlation. “The east and north positions move together during a turn.” “Vertical speed relates to height above threshold.” Explicitly represented as a 6 × 6 matrix you can print out — and in section 9 we do.

The FFN now does

temporal pattern extraction, per channel. It sees a whole series as one vector and learns what shapes recur — decelerating descent, a turn onto final — and how they continue.

Check your understanding

If you fed this model 20 channels instead of 6, how many parameters would it gain?

Show answer

Zero. Channels are tokens, and a Transformer's parameter count does not depend on how many tokens you give it — the same weights are applied to each. Only the shape of one token (60 numbers) and d_model enter the weight shapes. This is verified numerically in section 11; it is a genuinely unusual property that follows directly from the inversion.

Here is the actual source, abridged only by removing the use_norm branch (section 10):

# vendor/itransformer/model.py:42-71 — verbatim upstream, comments included
def forecast(self, x_enc, x_mark_enc, x_dec, x_mark_dec):
    _, _, N = x_enc.shape # B L N
    # B: batch_size;    E: d_model;
    # L: seq_len;       S: pred_len;
    # N: number of variate (tokens), can also includes covariates

    # Embedding
    # B L N -> B N E                (B L N -> B L E in the vanilla Transformer)
    enc_out = self.enc_embedding(x_enc, x_mark_enc)

    # B N E -> B N E
    # the dimensions of embedded time series has been inverted, and then processed by
    # native attn, layernorm and ffn modules
    enc_out, attns = self.encoder(enc_out, attn_mask=None)

    # B N E -> B N S -> B S N
    dec_out = self.projector(enc_out).permute(0, 2, 1)[:, :, :N]
    return dec_out, attns
Read those upstream comments carefully

B L N -> B N E  (B L E in the vanilla Transformer) — the authors are telling you exactly where the inversion happens and what it replaced. E is d_model, S is pred_len. This is the one comment in the file worth memorising.

5. Step 1 — the inverted embedding

The whole embedding module is 16 lines, and most of them are a branch this project never takes. 

# vendor/itransformer/embed.py:22-37 — verbatim upstream
class DataEmbedding_inverted(nn.Module):
    def __init__(self, c_in, d_model, embed_type='fixed', freq='h', dropout=0.1):
        super(DataEmbedding_inverted, self).__init__()
        self.value_embedding = nn.Linear(c_in, d_model)
        self.dropout = nn.Dropout(p=dropout)

    def forward(self, x, x_mark):
        x = x.permute(0, 2, 1)
        # x: [Batch Variate Time]
        if x_mark is None:
            x = self.value_embedding(x)
        else:
            # the potential to take covariates (e.g. timestamps) as tokens
            x = self.value_embedding(torch.cat([x, x_mark.permute(0, 2, 1)], 1))
        # x: [Batch Variate d_model]
        return self.dropout(x)

The one line that inverts everything

x.permute(0, 2, 1) turns [B, 60, 6] into [B, 6, 60]. That is it. That single transpose is the difference between a vanilla Transformer and an iTransformer. Everything downstream — attention, LayerNorm, FFN — is stock code that neither knows nor cares which axis it is operating on. 整个「倒置」在代码里就是这一行转置。

The naming trap — read this twice

The first parameter is called c_in. In every other embedding in the deep-learning world, c_in means “number of input channels”. Here it does not. Look at how it is constructed: 

# vendor/itransformer/model.py:22
self.enc_embedding = DataEmbedding_inverted(configs.seq_len, configs.d_model,
                                            configs.embed, configs.freq, configs.dropout)

It is passed seq_len = 60, not the channel count. So the layer is nn.Linear(60, 128): it maps a time series to an embedding, applied independently to each of the 6 channel-rows. Verified in the parameter dump — value_embedding.weight has shape (128, 60). If you ever find yourself confused about whether a shape is 6 or 60, this is almost always the reason. 这里的 c_in 是序列长度 60，不是通道数 6。

What one token now contains

Think of nn.Linear(60, 128) as 128 learned templates, each 60 steps long. The embedding of a channel is the list of 128 inner products between that channel's history and each template. One template might respond to “steady descent”, another to “decelerating”, another to “oscillating”. The resulting 128-vector is a summary of the whole history of one physical quantity. That is why it deserves to be a token — unlike a single time step, it means something.

Note what is missing

There is no positional encoding. A vanilla Transformer must add one, because attention is permutation-invariant and shuffling the time steps would otherwise be undetectable. Here, time order lives inside a token — it is the input order of the nn.Linear, which is a fixed weight per position and therefore inherently ordered. The tokens themselves (the 6 channels) genuinely have no meaningful order, which is exactly the situation attention was designed for. 不需要位置编码：时间顺序被编码在线性层的权重顺序里。

The x_mark branch is for calendar covariates (hour-of-day, day-of-week), which upstream attaches as additional tokens along the channel axis. This project passes None — time enters as a uniform 2 s grid, not as timestamps. models.py:39–41 That is also why the embed and freq arguments are accepted and then never used: the inverted embedding has no per-timestep slot to attach a calendar feature to. See section 10.

6. Step 2 — attention, built up slowly

We now have 6 tokens of 128 numbers each. This section builds attention from nothing. If you already know it, skip to 6.4 for the code mapping.

6.1 The idea in words

Each token wants to update itself using information from the other tokens — but not equally from all of them. It should decide, per input, whom to listen to. Attention implements exactly that in three moves:

  1. Every token emits a query (查询): “here is what I am looking for.”
  2. Every token emits a key (键): “here is what I contain.”
  3. Query i is compared against key j for all pairs. High similarity ⇒ token i pulls in a lot of token j's value (值).

Queries, keys and values are just three different linear projections of the same token vector. The comparison is a dot product; the weights are normalised to sum to 1 by a softmax.

6.2 The formula

Standard scaled dot-product attention, unmodified from the 2017 paper:

Attention(Q, K, V) = softmax( Q KT / √d ) V

With our shapes, per batch element:

QuantityShapeMeaning
Q, K, V6 × 128One row per channel token.
QKᵀ6 × 6Raw similarity between every pair of channels.
/ √d6 × 6Scaling. Without it, dot products of 128-dim vectors grow large, the softmax saturates, and gradients vanish. d = 16 here (128 ÷ 8 heads).
softmax(·)6 × 6Each row normalised to sum to 1 — this is the attention map.
(·) V6 × 128Weighted average of value vectors. Output: one refreshed vector per channel.

6.3 Multiple heads

Doing this once forces each channel to pick a single blend. Instead, n_heads = 8 copies run in parallel on 16-dimensional slices of the 128, and their outputs are concatenated. Each head can specialise — one might track horizontal geometry, another the vertical profile. 多头：把 128 维切成 8 份各 16 维，各自独立做注意力，最后拼接。 Note the slicing is free: d_keys = d_model // n_heads, so 8 heads of 16 cost exactly the same as 1 head of 128. That is why TSConfig.__post_init__ requires d_model % n_heads == 0. config.py:148–151

6.4 Mapping the formula onto the code

Two classes. AttentionLayer does the projections and head bookkeeping; FullAttention does the formula.

# vendor/itransformer/attention.py:66-100 — AttentionLayer (abridged)
d_keys = d_keys or (d_model // n_heads)          # 128 // 8 = 16
self.query_projection = nn.Linear(d_model, d_keys * n_heads)   # the three
self.key_projection   = nn.Linear(d_model, d_keys * n_heads)   # projections
self.value_projection = nn.Linear(d_model, d_values * n_heads)
self.out_projection   = nn.Linear(d_values * n_heads, d_model) # after concat

def forward(self, queries, keys, values, attn_mask, tau=None, delta=None):
    B, L, _ = queries.shape                       # here L is 6 — the CHANNEL count!
    queries = self.query_projection(queries).view(B, L, H, -1)   # split into heads
    keys    = self.key_projection(keys).view(B, S, H, -1)
    values  = self.value_projection(values).view(B, S, H, -1)
    out, attn = self.inner_attention(queries, keys, values, attn_mask, ...)
    out = out.view(B, L, -1)                      # concat heads back to 128
    return self.out_projection(out), attn
# vendor/itransformer/attention.py:44-63 — FullAttention.forward
B, L, H, E = queries.shape          # [batch, 6 channels, 8 heads, 16 dims]
scale = self.scale or 1. / sqrt(E)

scores = torch.einsum("blhe,bshe->bhls", queries, keys)   # Q Kᵀ  -> [B, 8, 6, 6]
A = self.dropout(torch.softmax(scale * scores, dim=-1))  # normalise each row
V = torch.einsum("bhls,bshd->blhd", A, values)            # weighted sum of values
Reading einsum

"blhe,bshe->bhls" looks cryptic but is mechanical. Each letter names an axis: b = batch, l = query token, s = key token, h = head, e = per-head dim. Letters appearing on both sides of the comma but not in the output are summed over — here that is e, the 16-dim head axis. So it reads: “for each batch, head, query token l and key token s, sum over the embedding dimension” — that is precisely a dot product per pair, i.e. QKT. Output [B, 8, 6, 6] — and this is exactly the shape measured in section 9.

Two variables named L that mean different things

In model.py, L means seq_len = 60. Inside attention.py and encoder.py, the same letter is the token axis, which after the inversion is 6. The attention code is generic — it was written for the vanilla layout and never told about the swap. This is the single most common source of confusion when reading the file; when in doubt, trace the tensor, not the letter.

Dead code you will trip over

FullAttention contains a causal-masking branch (attention.py:51–55) that is never taken here: the model constructs FullAttention(False, ...), so mask_flag is off. model.py:30 It would make no sense if it were on — masking enforces “cannot see the future”, but the tokens are channels, not time steps, and channel 3 is not “after” channel 2. Likewise tau and delta are threaded through every signature and never used; they belong to the Non-stationary Transformer this code lineage borrowed from. The class is kept byte-identical to upstream on purpose. vendor/itransformer/PROVENANCE.md

7. Step 3 — the rest of an encoder layer

Attention is one half of an encoder layer. Here is the whole thing, and it is short: 

# vendor/itransformer/encoder.py:38-50 — EncoderLayer.forward
def forward(self, x, attn_mask=None, tau=None, delta=None):
    new_x, attn = self.attention(x, x, x, attn_mask=attn_mask, tau=tau, delta=delta)
    x = x + self.dropout(new_x)                              # residual 1

    y = x = self.norm1(x)                                    # LayerNorm
    y = self.dropout(self.activation(self.conv1(y.transpose(-1, 1))))   # 128 -> 256
    y = self.dropout(self.conv2(y).transpose(-1, 1))                    # 256 -> 128

    return self.norm2(x + y), attn                           # residual 2 + LayerNorm

Why Conv1d for a feed-forward network?

conv1 and conv2 look like convolutions but have kernel_size=1. A 1×1 convolution is exactly a linear layer applied independently at each position — the same weights, just expecting the channel axis in a different place, which is why the code transposes around them. It is a stylistic habit from vision code, nothing more. kernel_size=1 的 1D 卷积等价于逐位置的全连接层。

The widths are d_model → d_ff → d_model = 128 → 256 → 128, with a GELU in between. Expanding then contracting is the standard design: the wide middle gives the nonlinearity room to work while the layer's input and output stay compatible for stacking.

A default this project overrides

encoder.py:29 reads d_ff = d_ff or 4 * d_model — upstream's convention is a 4× expansion (512 here). This project passes d_ff = 256 explicitly, i.e. 2×. That is a deliberate downsizing for a small dataset, but note it is an untested choice, unlike use_norm which has a measured ablation behind it. config.py:92

Residuals and LayerNorm

  • Residual (残差连接): x = x + sublayer(x). The sublayer only has to learn a correction, and gradients reach early layers through the addition without passing through any weights. This is what makes deep stacks trainable.
  • LayerNorm (层归一化): rescales each token's 128 numbers to zero mean and unit variance, then applies a learned scale and shift. It keeps activations in a sane range as layers stack.
  • This is post-norm — normalisation comes after the residual addition. Post-norm is the original 2017 arrangement; modern code often prefers pre-norm for training stability at greater depth. At 3 layers it makes little practical difference, but it is worth knowing which one you are reading. A final LayerNorm is also applied after the whole stack (model.py:38, applied at encoder.py:76–77).

8. Step 4 — the projector

One line builds it and one line uses it:

# vendor/itransformer/model.py:40
self.projector = nn.Linear(configs.d_model, configs.pred_len, bias=True)

# vendor/itransformer/model.py:64
dec_out = self.projector(enc_out).permute(0, 2, 1)[:, :, :N]
#         [B,6,128] -> [B,6,30]  -> permute -> [B,30,6]

Each channel's 128-dimensional token is mapped straight to that channel's entire future — all 30 (or 300) time steps in a single matrix multiply. This design has a name in the literature: direct multi-step forecasting (直接多步预测), as opposed to autoregressive generation.

Why this is good

  • No error accumulation within one call — step 300 is not computed from a predicted step 299.
  • One forward pass, so inference is fast and there is no decoder to train.
  • Every horizon step gets its own row of weights, so the model can learn that far-ahead steps are inherently more uncertain.

Why this costs something

  • The horizon is fixed at training time. Want 600 s instead of 60 s? Different weight shape, different model. That is precisely why this project has two horizon modes rather than one.
  • Nothing enforces smoothness or physical consistency between predicted steps — they are independent outputs of one linear layer.

The [:, :, :N] slice discards covariate tokens, if any were appended by the x_mark path. Here N is already 6 and nothing was appended, so it is a no-op — kept because the file is a verbatim copy.

Consequence for this project: no physics

The output is six numbers per step from a linear layer. Nothing in this architecture knows about lift, drag, bank-angle limits, or the fact that ∫ edot dt ought to equal e. A prediction can be smooth, plausible, and completely unflyable. That is a deliberate scope decision for this package — it is a kinematic baseline, measured by flyability.py but not corrected by it. See the package README's “what this is not” section before drawing conclusions from a prediction.

What the trained model actually learned

Layer 0 — no opinion yet. Every value sits between 0.151 and 0.184, against a uniform baseline of 0.167. The map is flat. The first layer is essentially averaging all channels together, building generic mixed features rather than committing to any relationship.

Layer 1 — the striking one. Look at the bottom-left block:

The model rediscovered calculus

Each derivative channel attends most strongly to its own position channel — edot→e, ndot→n, udot→u — and this was never told to it. Nothing in the loss, the architecture, or the channel names encodes that edot is the derivative of e. It is a pure statistical discovery from 702 training flights. 模型自己学到了「导数通道」与「位置通道」的配对关系——这正是 channels.py 里 ∫ edot dt = e 的构造性关系。

This is also the cleanest possible demonstration that the inversion buys something real. A channel-independent model cannot represent this relationship at all, no matter how long you train it — which is exactly the contrast drawn in the PatchTST tutorial.

Two more things in layer 1 that reward a second look:

  • The diagonal is dark, not bright. e→e is 0.124, ndot→ndot is 0.085 — well below uniform. Channels systematically look at others rather than themselves. That is not a bug: the residual connection (figure 4) already carries each token's own content forward untouched, so spending attention on yourself would be redundant. Attention is used for what the residual cannot supply.
  • u is the most-attended column overall. Both e (0.236) and n (0.223) attend to height above threshold more than to each other. Aviationally this is reasonable — on a stabilised approach, altitude is tightly coupled to distance-to-go, so height is an excellent proxy for “how far along the approach am I?”, which is what the horizontal channels most need to know.

Layer 2 — re-mixing. The sharp pairing has softened; the strongest cell is 0.218 and the map is broadly spread. A common pattern in deep stacks: middle layers specialise, later layers recombine into whatever representation the output projection wants.

Interpret attention maps carefully

Attention weights are suggestive, not proof of causal structure. They are averaged over heads here, which can hide heads that disagree; a large weight on a near-zero value vector moves nothing; and a model can route information through the residual stream instead. Treat figure 5 as strong evidence that cross-channel structure is being used — not as a complete account of the computation. 注意力权重是启发性证据，不是因果证明。

10. The parts this project switches off

10.1 Instance normalisation — use_norm = False

Upstream defaults this on. This project forces it off, and it matters more than any other knob.

# vendor/itransformer/model.py:43-48 and 66-69 — what use_norm=True would do
if self.use_norm:
    means = x_enc.mean(1, keepdim=True).detach()
    x_enc = x_enc - means                       # subtract this window's mean
    stdev = torch.sqrt(torch.var(x_enc, dim=1, keepdim=True, unbiased=False) + 1e-5)
    x_enc /= stdev
    # ... model runs ...
    dec_out = dec_out * stdev + means           # put the level back afterwards

The technique removes each window's absolute level before the model sees it, then restores it at the end. For electricity demand or stock prices this is sensible — the shape is signal, the level is drift you would rather not memorise.

Why it is backwards here

Our frame is anchored at the runway threshold, so absolute position is the signal. “15 km east at 900 m” and “3 km east at 200 m” are entirely different situations demanding different predictions; subtracting the window mean tells the model they are the same. 本项目的坐标原点就是跑道入口，绝对位置本身就是信息，减去均值等于把它扔掉。

The measured cost, ablated on real KRDU data across 8 cells: off wins 19 of 20 accuracy comparisons, with one tie. The signature of leaving it on is unmistakable — lateral p95 error pins at 14.28–14.50 km in all four cells, i.e. a model that cannot place the endpoint at all, versus 2.6–8.5 km with it off. outputs/KRDU/_ablation_norm/ablation_results.json

10.2 Knobs that are read but do nothing

Four config fields exist only so that TSConfig remains a drop-in replacement for upstream's argparse namespace. Model.__init__ reads them; nothing on this path acts on them.

embed"timeF"Selects a calendar-feature embedding style. DataEmbedding_inverted accepts the argument and never uses it (embed.py:23) — there is no per-timestep slot to attach one to. This project also passes x_mark=None.
freq"h"Would set how many calendar features exist. Same reason.
factor1A sampling factor for ProbAttention (the Informer variant), which was dropped from the vendoring entirely.

10.3 What “vendored” means here

The files under vendor/itransformer/ are copied from thuml/iTransformer at pinned commit c2426e68 (MIT). Two kinds of change were made, both mechanical:

  1. Import paths rewritten to relative (from .encoder import ...) — forced, because iTransformer and PatchTST both ship a layers/Embed.py with different contents and would collide on a top-level path.
  2. Unused classes dropped — the decoder, six unused embeddings, and the attention variants that would have dragged in reformer_pytorch and einops as dependencies.

Every remaining class body is byte-identical to upstream, so a future git diff against a newer commit stays readable. This is why oddities like the unused tau/delta arguments and the ignored embed_type are still there — they are upstream's, preserved deliberately rather than cleaned up.

11. Four consequences you can verify

All of the following were run against this repository while writing this document. Re-run them yourself — that is the point.

11.1 Parameter count is independent of the channel count

# iTransformer, window mode, varying only the number of channels
C =  2 -> 409,374 parameters
C =  3 -> 409,374
C =  6 -> 409,374
C = 20 -> 409,374

Identical, because channels are tokens. Compare with the vanilla layout, where the embedding is nn.Linear(C, d_model) and the count would grow with every added channel. Where the parameters actually live:

ComponentShapeParamsShare
Embedding Linear(60→128)(128, 60)7,8081.91 %
3 × attention (Q, K, V, out)4 × (128,128)198,14448.40 %
3 × FFN (conv1, conv2)(256,128,1), (128,256,1)197,76048.31 %
LayerNorms (3×2 + 1 final)(128,) weight + bias1,7920.44 %
Projector Linear(128→30)(30, 128)3,8700.95 %
Total (window mode)409,374100 %

Attention and the FFN each take roughly half; embedding and projector together are under 3 %. In full mode the projector becomes Linear(128→300) — 38,700 parameters, 8.7 % of a 444,204-parameter total. Everything else is byte-for-byte the same size.

11.2 The model accepts a different channel count at inference

# same trained-shape model, three different channel counts, no error
C =  3 -> output (2, 30, 3)
C =  6 -> output (2, 30, 6)
C = 11 -> output (2, 30, 11)

A vanilla Transformer would crash immediately — its embedding expects exactly C inputs. Here N is just “how many tokens I was handed”. Whether the predictions would be any good is a separate question (the normalizer statistics and the learned channel semantics would be wrong), but the architecture genuinely does not object. This is the property upstream leans on for zero-shot generalisation to unseen variables.

11.3 Extracting the attention maps yourself

# from the repo root, with the aeroviz env active:
#   source scripts/activate_aeroviz_env.sh && aeroviz_activate_env
#   export PYTHONPATH=$PWD:$PWD/4dTrajectory/ts_transformer
import torch
from dataclasses import replace
from config import TSConfig
from dataset import load_flight_dicts, build_series, split_by_flight, TrajectoryWindows, Normalizer
from models import build_model

p   = torch.load("4dTrajectory/outputs/KRDU/ts_itransformer_full/checkpoint.pt",
                 map_location="cpu", weights_only=True)
cfg = TSConfig.from_dict(p["config"])
model = build_model(replace(cfg, output_attention=True))   # a flag, not a weight
model.load_state_dict(p["model_state"]); model.eval()

flights   = load_flight_dicts("trajectory_data_process/outputs/landings/KRDU")
series, _ = build_series(flights, cfg, airport="KRDU", aircraft_type=cfg.aircraft_type)
_, _, test = split_by_flight(series, cfg)
ds = TrajectoryWindows(test, cfg, Normalizer.from_dict(p["normalizer"]))

x = torch.stack([ds[i][0] for i in range(0, len(ds), 50)])[:512]
with torch.no_grad():
    _, attns = model.inner(x, None, None, None)   # list of [B, heads, 6, 6], one per layer
print(attns[1].mean(dim=(0, 1)))   # layer 1, averaged -> the 6x6 map in figure 5

13. Where this leaves us

iTransformer's strengths here

  • Cross-channel coupling is representable and demonstrably used (figure 5) — the east/north/up geometry of a turn is exactly this kind of relationship.
  • Parameter count independent of channel count; accepts variable channel counts at inference.
  • No positional encoding to get wrong; time order is structural.
  • Measured on this data: leads at long prediction lead times.

Its limits here

  • Only 6 tokens — a very short sequence for attention to work with. Most capacity sits in the FFN.
  • Temporal detail is compressed into 128 dims by a single linear layer; fine structure can be lost.
  • Fixed horizon baked into the projector.
  • Measured on this data: loses at short lead times to PatchTST.

That last pair of bullets is the hook for the companion document. PatchTST answers the token question a third way — cut the time axis into patches, and forecast every channel in complete isolation. It is in most respects the philosophical opposite of what you just read, and on this dataset each model wins at a different time scale.
