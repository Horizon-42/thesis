1. Where we are: the same task, a third answer

Identical setup to the companion document, so this is a recap in one paragraph. The model is shown seq_len = 60 samples on a 2-second grid — 120 seconds of observed track — as six channels in a runway-threshold-anchored ENU frame: e, n, u (metres east/north/up) and edot, ndot, udot (their time derivatives). It must output the next pred_len samples: 30 (60 s, window mode) or 300 (600 s, full mode). 

model(x: Tensor[B, 60, 6]) -> Tensor[B, H, 6]

# verified by running it:
patchtst/window: in (4, 60, 6) -> out (4,  30, 6) | 427,422 parameters
patchtst/full  : in (4, 60, 6) -> out (4, 300, 6) | 669,612 parameters

Same input, same output, same wrapper interface. Everything that follows is about what happens in between. Recall the question that decides a Transformer's architecture:

The token question, once more

Given a 60 × 6 table of numbers, what should one token be? 给定一个「60 时刻 × 6 变量」的数值表，什么才应该是一个 token？

AnswerOne token is…Token countAttention compares
Vanilla Transformerall 6 channels at one time step60time step ↔ time step
iTransformerone channel's whole 60-step series6channel ↔ channel
PatchTSTa 16-step slice of one channel7 (per channel)time slice ↔ time slice, within one channel

PatchTST sits between the other two. It keeps attention along the time axis — like the vanilla model — but makes the units bigger, and it never lets two channels meet.

2. Two independent ideas, not one

This is the single most useful framing to hold onto, and it is easy to miss because the paper presents them together. PatchTST is two separate design decisions bolted onto an otherwise ordinary Transformer encoder. They are logically independent — you could adopt either without the other.

Idea 1 · Patching 分块

Group neighbouring time steps into overlapping patches, and make each patch a token.

Buys: tokens that carry local shape instead of a single instant; far fewer of them, so attention is cheap; a longer lookback becomes affordable.

Idea 2 · Channel independence 通道独立

Forecast every channel separately, with shared weights. No cross-channel path exists.

Buys: 6× more training sequences from the same data; strong regularisation. Costs: cross-channel relationships become unrepresentable, not merely unlearned.

Keep them separate in your head, because they have different consequences and, in this project, land on opposite sides of the ledger. Patching is straightforwardly good here. Channel independence is the reason PatchTST loses to iTransformer at long lead times on turning aircraft — and section 9 shows that limitation directly in the trained model's own attention.

3. Idea 1 — patching

Why a single time step is a bad token

On a 2-second grid, one sample of a smooth approach differs from its neighbour by a fraction of a percent. As a unit of meaning it is nearly empty, and 60 near-identical tokens give attention little to discriminate between. The fix is the same one Vision Transformers apply to images: don't tokenise pixels, tokenise patches. A 16-step window carries something a single step cannot — a local trend, a curvature, a rate of change. 把相邻时刻打包成「块」，一个 token 才携带局部形状信息。

The arithmetic

Three config fields control it, and one line of code computes the result:

config.py:115–117 — patch_len, stride, padding_patch backbone.py:35–38 — patch_num 
# vendor/patchtst/backbone.py:35-38
patch_num = int((context_window - patch_len)/stride + 1)      # (60-16)/8 + 1 = 6
if padding_patch == 'end':
    self.padding_patch_layer = nn.ReplicationPad1d((0, stride))
    patch_num += 1                                            # -> 7
SettingValueMeaning
patch_len16Samples per patch = 32 seconds of flight.
stride8Step between patch starts = 16 s. Half of patch_len, so patches overlap by 50 %.
padding_patch"end"Replication-pads 8 samples at the end so one extra patch fits, and the final samples appear in two patches rather than being under-represented.
resultpatch_num = 760 time steps become 7 tokens per channel.
A padding detail worth knowing

nn.ReplicationPad1d((0, stride)) repeats the last observed value 8 times. So patch 6 is roughly three-quarters real data and one-quarter a frozen copy of the anchor sample. That is benign for a smooth trajectory, but it does mean the final patch is slightly “flattened” relative to the others — worth remembering when you see in section 9 that patch 6 is one of the two most-attended tokens. 最后一块有约 1/4 是复制填充，不是真实数据。

Why this matters computationally

Attention cost grows with the square of the token count. Patching cuts 60 tokens to 7:

TokenisationTokensAttention matrixRelative cost
vanilla (per time step)6060 × 60 = 360073×
PatchTST (per patch)77 × 7 = 491×
iTransformer (per channel)66 × 6 = 360.73×

In the original paper that saving is what makes long lookbacks (336 or 512 steps) practical, and long lookbacks are where PatchTST's benchmark wins come from. Here it barely matters — a 60-step lookback was never expensive. Patching earns its place in this project for the semantic reason (better tokens), not the computational one. Do not carry the paper's efficiency argument over to this setting without noticing that it does not apply.

4. Idea 2 — channel independence

This is the more consequential half, and it is implemented by a single reshape. 

# vendor/patchtst/backbone.py:157-169 — TSTiEncoder.forward   ("i" = channel-independent)
n_vars = x.shape[1]
x = x.permute(0,1,3,2)                             # [bs, nvars, patch_num, patch_len]
x = self.W_P(x)                                    # [bs, nvars, patch_num, d_model]

u = torch.reshape(x, (x.shape[0]*x.shape[1], x.shape[2], x.shape[3]))
#     [bs, nvars, patch_num, d_model]  ->  [bs * nvars, patch_num, d_model]
u = self.dropout(u + self.W_pos)

z = self.encoder(u)                                # [bs * nvars, patch_num, d_model]
z = torch.reshape(z, (-1, n_vars, z.shape[-2], z.shape[-1]))   # split channels back out

bs * nvars. The channel axis is folded into the batch axis. From the encoder's point of view it is not receiving a batch of 4 six-channel flights; it is receiving a batch of 24 independent single-channel sequences. It has no way to tell which of them came from the same aircraft. 通道维被折进了批次维——编码器看到的是 24 条互不相关的单通道序列。

The trade, stated plainly

The gain. Every channel is another training sequence for the same weights. On 702 KRDU training flights that is 4 212 channel-sequences instead of 702 six-channel ones. For a small dataset this is real, and it is why PatchTST generalises well on benchmarks with many series.

The loss. Cross-channel structure is architecturally unrepresentable. Train it for a thousand epochs on ten million flights and it still cannot express “when the aircraft banks left, e and n change together in a specific coupled way”. iTransformer's measured layer-1 attention — each derivative channel locking onto its own position channel — is exactly the kind of thing that cannot happen here. 通道间的耦合不是「没学到」，而是「表达不了」。

Check your understanding

If channels never interact, how does the model produce six output channels at all?

Show answer

Each of the 24 sequences is forecast on its own, and the results are stacked back into [bs, nvars, H] at backbone.py:169. Channel e's forecast is computed only from channel e's history — using weights that were also trained on n, u and the rest. Shared parameters, separate computations. That distinction is the whole of channel independence.

6. Patch embedding and positional encoding

# vendor/patchtst/backbone.py:142-147 — TSTiEncoder.__init__
q_len = patch_num                                     # 7
self.W_P = nn.Linear(patch_len, d_model)              # 16 -> 128
self.W_pos = positional_encoding(pe, learn_pe, q_len, d_model)   # (7, 128), learned

W_P is a single Linear(16, 128) — 2 176 parameters, 0.5 % of the model — applied to every patch of every channel. It is the direct analogue of a Vision Transformer's patch embedding. Its tiny size is worth noticing: unlike iTransformer, whose embedding must ingest a whole 60-step series, PatchTST's only has to encode 16 numbers.

Here, positional encoding is required

This is a genuine structural difference from the companion model, and a good check on whether the “tokens are a set” idea has landed.

iTransformer — none needed

Tokens are channels. They have no natural order, and shuffling them is harmless. Time order lives inside a token, as the input order of a Linear(60, 128).

PatchTST — required

Tokens are time slices, and their order is the chronology. Without a position signal, attention could not tell patch 0 (two minutes ago) from patch 6 (now) — the forecast would be direction-blind.

So a learned (7, 128) table is added to the patch embeddings (backbone.py:165): 896 parameters, initialised from zeros (pe='zeros') and trained (learn_pe=True). PatchTST 必须有位置编码，因为它的 token 就是时间顺序本身。

7. Inside the encoder — three real differences

The encoder layer is the familiar rhythm: attention → add & norm → feed-forward → add & norm (backbone.py:237–267). Three details genuinely differ from iTransformer's, and all three are easy to miss because they are constructor defaults rather than config fields.

7.1 BatchNorm, not LayerNorm

# vendor/patchtst/backbone.py:215-218
if "batch" in norm.lower():
    self.norm_attn = nn.Sequential(Transpose(1,2), nn.BatchNorm1d(d_model), Transpose(1,2))
else:
    self.norm_attn = nn.LayerNorm(d_model)

norm defaults to 'BatchNorm' (model.py:16) and this project never overrides it — verified by printing the module. The two normalise along different axes: LayerNorm standardises each token across its own 128 features; BatchNorm standardises each feature across the whole batch. Because channels are folded into the batch here, that batch is 24 sequences wide even at batch_size=4 — BatchNorm gets healthy statistics.

BatchNorm has state; LayerNorm does not

BatchNorm1d keeps running mean and variance buffers and behaves differently in train() and eval() mode. iTransformer, being pure LayerNorm, does not care. So model.eval() matters more for PatchTST than for its sibling — forget it and predictions shift. train.py and load_checkpoint handle this (train.py:268), but any hand-written inference script must too. BatchNorm 带运行统计量，务必 model.eval()。

7.2 Residual attention across layers

# vendor/patchtst/backbone.py:354-357 — _ScaledDotProductAttention.forward
attn_scores = torch.matmul(q, k) * self.scale
if prev is not None: attn_scores = attn_scores + prev   # scores from the PREVIOUS layer

With res_attention=True (the default, model.py:17), each layer adds the previous layer's pre-softmax scores to its own before normalising, and passes its scores onward (backbone.py:192–194). This is the “RealFormer” trick: a residual connection for the attention pattern itself, not just for the token values. It tends to make attention maps sharper and more consistent across depth — a detail that will be visible in the measured maps.

7.3 A frozen scale parameter

# vendor/patchtst/backbone.py:335
self.scale = nn.Parameter(torch.tensor(head_dim ** -0.5), requires_grad=lsa)   # lsa=False

The 1/√d factor is stored as a Parameter with requires_grad=False — it exists so that “locality self-attention” could make it learnable, which this project does not enable. It is a harmless curiosity with one visible consequence: the model has 427 425 parameters but only 427 422 trainable ones. The three-parameter gap is these frozen scales, one per layer. If a parameter count ever looks three off, this is why.

8. The flatten head

# vendor/patchtst/backbone.py:106-107 and 120-122
self.flatten = nn.Flatten(start_dim=-2)
self.linear  = nn.Linear(nf, target_window)     # nf = d_model * patch_num = 128 * 7 = 896

def forward(self, x):        # x: [bs, nvars, d_model, patch_num]
    x = self.flatten(x)      # [bs, nvars, 896]
    x = self.linear(x)       # [bs, nvars, H]

All 7 patch tokens of a channel are concatenated into one 896-dimensional vector, and a single linear layer maps that to the channel's entire future. Like iTransformer's projector this is direct multi-step forecasting — no autoregression, no decoder, horizon fixed at training time.

One consequence is sharply different, though. This head is where PatchTST's parameters go when the horizon grows:

Modeheadhead paramstotalhead share
window (H = 30)Linear(896 → 30)26,910427,4226.3 %
full (H = 300)Linear(896 → 300)269,100669,61240.2 %

In full mode two fifths of the whole model is that one output layer. iTransformer's projector maps from 128 rather than 896, so its equivalent growth is 3 870 → 38 700 — a total of 444 204 against PatchTST's 669 612. Same task, same horizon, 1.5× the parameters, almost all of it in the head.

Why individual = False matters

Flatten_Head can build a separate head per channel (backbone.py:97–104). This project leaves individual = False, so all six channels share one head — consistent with channel independence, and it keeps the parameter count down. Turning it on would multiply the head by six (in full mode: +1.35 M parameters) and give each channel its own decoder while still never letting them interact.

Finding 1 — the model looks at the endpoints and ignores the middle

Interpretation

For position channels, two of seven patches carry ~56 % of the attention — nearly double the 28.6 % they would get under uniform attention. Those two are “where the window began” and “where the aircraft is now”. That is a displacement estimator: subtract the start from the present and you have the average velocity across the whole 120 s window, which for a smooth approach is by far the most useful summary. The middle patches are largely redundant precisely because the path is smooth — they are interpolations of the endpoints. 模型学会了「看头看尾」——两端相减就是整个窗口的平均速度。

The gradient down each endpoint column is a nice confirmation: in channel e, the weight on p6 rises from .281 (query p0) to .363 (query p5), i.e. later patches lean harder on the present. Meanwhile udot — the noisiest, least trend-like channel — stays essentially uniform (column means .133–.152, versus 0.143 uniform). Where there is no clean trend to extract, the model extracts none.

Finding 2 — east and north get almost identical treatment

Compare the e and n maps by eye: they are nearly the same picture. Quantified as mean absolute difference per cell, over layer 2:

This is channel independence, made visible

East and north are treated as interchangeable — the same kind of smooth positional trend, given the same attention pattern to within half a percent per cell. And on a straight-in approach that is perfectly correct.

But a turn is precisely the event where it is wrong. When an aircraft banks onto final, east and north stop being two independent trends and become one rotating vector: e decelerating while n accelerates, in a coupled, geometrically constrained way. PatchTST processes them in separate forward passes and cannot condition either on the other. Contrast the companion document's figure 5, where iTransformer's layer 1 puts 0.303 of edot's attention on e and 0.305 of ndot's on n — a cross-channel relationship that has no expressible form here. 直线飞行时这样没问题，转弯时东和北是耦合的——而这里表达不了。

This is not a post-hoc story. It is the mechanism the package README gives for the measured outcome, and section 12 shows the numbers: the two models are near-tied at short lead times, and the gap opens as the horizon extends and the turn develops.

Finding 3 — layer 0 is nearly flat, and the sharpening is progressive

Click through layers 0 → 1 → 2. Layer 0 spans only 0.127–0.184 against a 0.143 baseline: barely any preference, just a mild lean toward recent patches. Layer 1 has the endpoint structure clearly. Layer 2 sharpens it further (channel e's strongest cell goes 0.322 → 0.363). That monotone sharpening with depth is the residual-attention mechanism from §7.2 doing its job — each layer adds to the previous layer's scores rather than starting over. iTransformer, which has no such mechanism, showed the opposite shape: specialise at layer 1, then re-mix at layer 2.

Same caution as before

Attention weights are suggestive, not proof. These are averaged over 8 heads, which can hide disagreement between them; a large weight on a near-zero value vector moves nothing; and the residual stream carries information that bypasses attention entirely. Read figure 3 as strong evidence about what the model finds salient — not as a complete account of the computation.

10. Switched off, and dead options

10.1 RevIN — the same decision as use_norm

PatchTST's per-window instance normalisation is called RevIN (reversible instance normalisation). It is on upstream and off here, for exactly the reason given in the companion document: our frame is anchored at the runway threshold, so a window's absolute level is the signal, and stripping it tells the model that “15 km out at 900 m” and “3 km out at 200 m” are the same situation. 

# vendor/patchtst/backbone.py:62-65 and 78-81 — what revin=True would do
if self.revin:
    z = z.permute(0,2,1); z = self.revin_layer(z, 'norm');   z = z.permute(0,2,1)
    # ... model runs ...
    z = z.permute(0,2,1); z = self.revin_layer(z, 'denorm'); z = z.permute(0,2,1)

10.3 What “vendored” means here

Files under vendor/patchtst/ are copied from yuqinie98/PatchTST at pinned commit 204c21ef (Apache-2.0), from the PatchTST_supervised/ tree. All four files were copied whole; the only change was rewriting imports to relative form, forced because this repo also vendors iTransformer and both ship a layers/Embed.py with different contents. Nothing was reformatted, renamed, or behaviourally edited — which is why the odd d_ff:int=256-style annotations and the unused lsa plumbing are still there. vendor/patchtst/PROVENANCE.md

One integration wrinkle is worth knowing: the vendored Model.__init__ reads most knobs off configs but takes the activation as a bare keyword argument. models.py:58 plumbs it explicitly — otherwise TSConfig.activation would silently apply to iTransformer only, while the checkpoint recorded it for both.

12.3 Why the crossover happens where it does

Short lead times are dominated by where the aircraft is and how it is moving right now — local, per-channel, temporal information. That is exactly what patch tokens encode well, and PatchTST's finer temporal resolution (7 tokens along time versus iTransformer's single compressed vector) wins.

Long lead times require anticipating the turn onto final: the aircraft's heading will change, and when it does, east and north evolve as one coupled rotating quantity. That is cross-channel structure. iTransformer represents it — and figure 5 of the companion document shows it doing so. PatchTST cannot, and pays for it as the horizon extends. Straight flight costs it nothing; the deficit only accrues once the turn develops.

Do not over-read a single margin

The package's own standing caution applies: treat any single-split margin under ~1.5× as provisional — that is the size of effect a train/test split change has already moved in this codebase. The 600 s direction has held across three training generations (6135/7142, 5407/6962, 5438/7384), which is what makes it quotable; each individual margin (1.16–1.36×) sits under that band on its own. The short-lead direction is the weaker of the two claims. 单次实验中小于约 1.5 倍的差距应视为暂定结论。

12.4 A result that is not about accuracy at all

Worth ending on, because it is the easiest way to draw a wrong conclusion from this package. flyability.py checks whether a predicted trajectory is inside the aircraft's flight envelope. On the observed real tracks the check scores 63.2 % — that is the floor, not 100 %, because the closed-form inversion judges against a clean-configuration drag polar while real approaches are flown dirty. Against that baseline:

Chained window mode is the most flyable and among the least accurate at the threshold. Chaining produces smooth, conservative, bland paths — and a straight line is perfectly flyable and completely wrong. Flyability alone is not a quality metric; always read it beside the error columns. Neither model passes the 106.75 m lateral gate in any meaningful number of cases (0/152 here), which is the expected result for a purely kinematic baseline with no dynamics attached — by design.

14. Where this leaves you

PatchTST's strengths here

  • Finer temporal resolution — 7 tokens along time, not one compressed vector.
  • Best short-lead accuracy of the two (478 m at 10 s).
  • Channel independence is a real regulariser: 6× the training sequences from the same flights.
  • Cheap attention; the design scales to much longer lookbacks than this project uses.

Its limits here

  • Cross-channel coupling is unrepresentable — measured as e≈n at 0.0045 per cell.
  • Loses at long lead, where the turn onto final dominates (1.36× at 600 s).
  • Full-mode head is 40 % of the parameters; 1.5× iTransformer's total.
  • BatchNorm state makes eval() discipline matter.
  • Least flyable of the four cells (25.0 % full mode).

Both models share the same deeper limitation, and it is the right note to end a pair of tutorials on: neither knows any physics. Six numbers in, six numbers out, fitted to observed tracks. No lift, no drag, no bank-angle limit, no guarantee that ∫ edot dt equals e. That is a deliberate scope decision — it is what lets the learned component be measured on its own terms — and the four routes to changing it (post-hoc flyability check, post-hoc casadi projection, soft physical loss, differentiable torch dynamics) are written up in the package README rather than half-built here.
