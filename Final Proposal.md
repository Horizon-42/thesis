# Physics-Informed and Constraint-Aware 4D Approach Trajectory Prediction

Approach procedures are safety-critical in aircraft operations. According to IATA statistics, at least 37.7% of aviation accidents directly involve issues with flight path control, energy management, or procedural execution during the approach and landing phases. This highlights the need for accurate and physically reliable modeling of approach trajectories, especially under operational constraints.

This research aims to develop a physics-informed and constraint-aware Transformer-based generative framework for 4D approach trajectory prediction. Instead of directly predicting future positions, the framework will predict aircraft control parameters and propagate them through a realistic aerodynamic model. The generated trajectories will then be evaluated under multi-level operational constraints, including spatial terrain boundaries, procedural RNAV minima, and dynamic multi-aircraft separation requirements.

Recent AI-driven 4D trajectory prediction methods generally follow four directions: deterministic sequence prediction, probabilistic or generative modeling, explicit use of physical and procedural constraints, and context- or intent-conditioned prediction. LSTM, TCN, and other sequence models are effective for short-term prediction, while GMMs, VAEs, and diffusion models better capture uncertainty and multimodal terminal operations. Constraint-aware methods further improve realism by incorporating flight dynamics, runway alignment, waypoints, or altitude restrictions. However, most existing methods still predict future positions directly and do not explicitly model aircraft control behavior, aerodynamic feasibility, or procedure-level constraints.

To address these limitations, the proposed framework has three core components. First, a Transformer predicts control parameters such as thrust and lift, which are used by a differentiable kinematics model to generate realistic 4D trajectories. Second, physical boundaries and operational rules are encoded as differentiable penalty functions to improve safety and compliance. Third, Graph Neural Networks or Reinforcement Learning are used for multi-aircraft conflict resolution in dense traffic, supporting safe separation and improved airspace efficiency.

For the experimental evaluation, historical ADS-B flight data from the OpenSky Network will be processed through normalization and denoising. High-precision airport terrain data will be sourced from USGS digital elevation models. The framework will be compared with up to three selected baselines from two model categories: deterministic sequence models, such as LSTM, TCN, or Transformer, and probabilistic models, such as GMM or VAE.

Performance will be evaluated using five metrics across three dimensions: prediction accuracy, measured by lateral and vertical errors; compliance, measured by adherence to flight regulations and kinematics constraints; and multi-aircraft safety, measured by the ability to maintain safe separation.
