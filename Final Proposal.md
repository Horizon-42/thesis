## Constrained 4D Approach Control via Generative Modeling
Candiate Title:
**Constrained 4D Approach Trajectory Management via Generative Modeling**

Approach operations in terminal airspace are safety-critical phases of flight. IATA (International Air Transport Association) safety data show that flight-path management, manual control, and SOP (Standard Operating Procedures) non-compliance remain recurring contributing factors in aviation accidents. These factors point to the need for approach trajectory models that are accurate, physically reliable, and consistent with operational constraints.

This research aims to develop a physics-informed and constraint-aware generative learning framework for 4D approach trajectory prediction. Instead of directly predicting future positions, the framework will predict aircraft control parameters and propagate them through a realistic aerodynamic model. The generated trajectories will then be evaluated under multi-level operational constraints, including spatial terrain boundaries, procedural RNAV minima, and dynamic multi-aircraft separation requirements.

Before learning-based methods, aircraft trajectory prediction mainly relied on model-based approaches grounded in physical and operational knowledge. Optimization-based methods further formulated trajectory prediction as a constrained generation problem, balancing feasibility, safety, and operational efficiency.
As data availability and modeling capacity have increased, recent AI-driven methods have shifted toward learning trajectory patterns directly from historical operations. These methods for 4D trajectory prediction generally fall into two categories: deterministic sequence models, such as LSTM and TCN, and probabilistic/generative models, such as GMMs, VAEs, and diffusion models. In terminal-airspace applications, these models are often conditioned on runway configuration, weather, or traffic states to better capture uncertainty and multimodal behavior. However, most still predict future trajectory coordinates directly, with limited explicit consideration of physical feasibility and procedural compliance.

The proposed framework addresses these limitations through three core components. First, a generative model predicts aircraft control parameters, which are then used by a differentiable kinematics model to generate realistic 4D (Four-Dimensional) trajectories. Second, physical boundaries and operational rules are encoded as differentiable penalty functions to improve safety and compliance. Third, multi-agent coordination methods are used for multi-aircraft conflict resolution in dense traffic to maintain safe separation and improve airspace efficiency.

The experimental evaluation will use historical ADS-B (Automatic Dependent Surveillance–Broadcast) flight data from the OpenSky Network, together with high-resolution airport terrain data from USGS (United States Geological Survey) digital elevation models. The ADS-B data will be normalized and denoised before model training and evaluation. 

The proposed framework will be evaluated against representative baselines from three categories: traditional optimization-based methods, deterministic sequence models, and probabilistic/generative models. Performance will be evaluated across three dimensions: arrival-time performance, which evaluates approach completion and time efficiency against historical trajectories with matched initial conditions and baseline methods; operational compliance, which evaluates violations of kinematic constraints and flight regulations; and multi-aircraft safety, which assesses separation losses and minimum separation margins.
