## Constrained 4D Approach Control via Generative Modeling

Approach operations in terminal airspace are safety-critical. IATA (International Air Transport Association) safety data show that flight-path management, manual control, and SOP (Standard Operating Procedures) non-compliance remain recurring contributing factors in aviation accidents. These factors point to the need for approach trajectory models that are accurate, physically reliable, and consistent with operational constraints.

This research aims to develop a physics-informed and constraint-aware generative learning framework for 4D approach trajectory prediction. Instead of forecasting future coordinates directly, the framework generates trajectories from aircraft control parameters and explicitly evaluates them using physical, procedural, and multi-aircraft separation constraints.

Before learning-based methods, aircraft trajectory prediction mainly relied on model-based approaches grounded in physical and operational knowledge. Optimization-based methods further solved trajectory prediction under explicit constraints, balancing feasibility, safety, and operational efficiency.

With increased data availability and modeling capacity, recent AI-driven methods have shifted toward learning trajectory patterns directly from historical data, ranging from deterministic sequence models such as long short-term memory networks and temporal convolutional networks to probabilistic or generative models such as Gaussian mixture models, variational autoencoders, and diffusion models. In terminal-airspace applications, these models often use runway configuration, weather, and surrounding traffic conditions as contextual inputs to account for context-dependent variability and, in probabilistic or generative settings, multimodal trajectory uncertainty. However, most existing methods still predict future trajectory coordinates directly, relying on data-driven pattern learning rather than explicitly enforcing physical feasibility and procedural compliance.

The proposed framework addresses these limitations through three core components. First, a generative model predicts aircraft control parameters, which are then used by a differentiable kinematics model to generate realistic 4D trajectories. Second, physical boundaries and operational rules are encoded as differentiable penalty functions to improve safety and compliance. Third, multi-agent coordination methods are used to resolve conflicts among multiple aircraft in dense traffic, helping maintain safe separation and improve airspace efficiency.

The experimental evaluation will use historical ADS-B (Automatic Dependent Surveillance–Broadcast) flight data from the OpenSky Network, together with high-resolution airport terrain data from USGS (United States Geological Survey) digital elevation models. The ADS-B data will be normalized and denoised before model training and evaluation. 

The framework will be evaluated against representative baselines from three categories: traditional optimization-based methods, deterministic sequence models, and probabilistic/generative models. Performance will be evaluated across three dimensions: arrival-time performance, which evaluates approach completion and time efficiency against historical trajectories with matched initial conditions and baseline methods; operational compliance, which evaluates violations of kinematic constraints and flight regulations; and multi-aircraft safety, which assesses separation losses and minimum separation margins.
