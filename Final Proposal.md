## Physics-Informed Generative Modeling for Constrained 4D Approach Trajectory Prediction

Approach operations in terminal airspace are safety-critical. IATA (International Air Transport Association) safety data identify flight-path management, manual control, and SOP (Standard Operating Procedures) non-compliance as recurring contributors to aviation accidents. These risks motivate accurate, reliable, and operationally compliant models for approach trajectory prediction.

This research develops a physics-informed, constraint-aware generative framework for 4D approach trajectory prediction. Instead of directly forecasting coordinates, it generates feasible trajectories from aircraft control parameters and evaluates them against physical, procedural, and separation constraints.

Aircraft trajectory prediction has evolved from physics- and optimization-based methods to AI-driven models that learn trajectory patterns from historical data. Recent approaches include deterministic, probabilistic, and generative models, often conditioned on runway configuration, weather, and traffic to capture context-dependent and multimodal behavior in terminal airspace. However, most still predict future coordinates directly, relying on implicit pattern learning rather than explicit enforcement of physical feasibility and procedural compliance.

The proposed framework addresses these limitations through three core components. First, a generative model predicts aircraft control parameters, which are then used by a differentiable kinematics model to generate realistic 4D trajectories. Second, physical boundaries and operational rules are encoded as differentiable penalty functions to improve safety and compliance. Third, multi-agent coordination methods are used to resolve conflicts among multiple aircraft in dense traffic, helping maintain safe separation and improve airspace efficiency.

The experimental evaluation will use historical ADS-B (Automatic Dependent Surveillance–Broadcast) flight data from the OpenSky Network, together with high-resolution airport terrain data from USGS (United States Geological Survey) digital elevation models. The ADS-B data will be normalized and denoised before model training and evaluation.

The framework will be compared with representative baselines from three categories: traditional optimization-based methods, deterministic sequence models, and probabilistic/generative models. Performance will be assessed along three dimensions: arrival-time performance, measuring approach completion and time efficiency against matched historical trajectories and baselines; operational compliance, measuring violations of kinematic constraints and flight regulations; and multi-aircraft safety, measuring separation losses and minimum separation margins.
